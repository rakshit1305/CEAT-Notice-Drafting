# Cease and Desist Notice

> **NON-STANDARD — no CEAT sample provided yet.** Built from standard legal structure +
> CEAT house style. Draft output must be marked "NON-STANDARD — for legal review" and
> validated by CEAT's legal team. Replace with CEAT's approved wording once a sample or
> an approved precedent is provided.
>
> **This type carries more legal risk than the others.** A cease and desist asserts a
> proprietary right and threatens relief on it. The right asserted, the statute cited and
> the relief threatened are matters for counsel, not for the drafter. Three things in
> particular must be settled by CEAT's legal team before this type is used on a live
> matter: (a) whether the notice issues from CEAT directly or through its advocates,
> which changes the whole signature block and closing; (b) which registrations CEAT
> relies on for the marks in question, with numbers and classes; (c) whether a
> cross-undertaking or an offer to settle should appear at all.

## When to use
A third party — a former dealer, an unauthorised seller, a counterfeiter, an ex-employee,
a competitor — is doing something CEAT has the right to stop: using CEAT's trademarks,
trade name or get-up; passing off goods as CEAT's; selling counterfeit or spurious
product; holding out as an authorised CEAT dealer after termination; using CEAT's
confidential information, drawings or customer data; retaining signage after a dealership
has ended. CEAT wants it to stop, wants a written undertaking that it has stopped, and
wants the record for any proceedings that follow.

Do **not** use this type where the complaint is simply non-payment (use Recovery or
Section 138) or a breach of a subsisting agreement that CEAT wants cured rather than
stopped (use Breach). A cease and desist is for conduct CEAT wants to end, not for an
obligation CEAT wants performed.

## Required inputs (ask for any that are missing)
- Other party: individual/proprietor OR company + directors OR partnership + partners;
  name(s) and address(es). Where the infringer is a shop or an online seller, the
  operator's own name as well as the trading name.
- The right CEAT asserts, and how CEAT holds it — registered trademark (with number and
  class if available), trade name, copyright, confidential information, or a surviving
  post-termination clause of an agreement with its date and clause number.
- The offending acts, with particulars: what is being done, where, since when, and how it
  came to CEAT's notice. An investigator's report, invoice, photograph, listing URL or
  test purchase is what makes the notice credible — identify it if there is one.
- Each act that must stop.
- The period within which it must stop and a written undertaking must reach CEAT.
- Consequences if it does not.
- Whether CEAT also requires delivery-up or destruction of infringing material, an
  account of profits, or disclosure of the source of the goods.
- Place of jurisdiction.
- Signatory, and whether the notice issues from CEAT or through its advocates.

## Template
```
{{LETTERHEAD}}

{{DATE}}
THROUGH SPEED POST / EMAIL / WHATSAPP
WITHOUT PREJUDICE

To,
{{NOTICEE_BLOCK}}

Sub: Cease and desist — unauthorised use of the Company's {{RIGHT_SHORT}}.

Sir/Madam,

1. CEAT Limited ("the Company") is a Public Limited Company incorporated under the
   Companies Act, 1956, having its Registered Office at 463, Dr. Annie Besant Road,
   Worli, Mumbai – 400030, engaged in the manufacture and sale of Tyres, Tubes and Flaps.

2. The Company is the proprietor of and exclusively entitled to {{RIGHT_ASSERTED}}. The
   said rights are valid, subsisting and enforceable, and the Company has not authorised
   you to use them in any manner.

3. The Company has learnt that {{OFFENDING_ACTS}}. The said acts came to the Company's
   notice on {{FIRST_NOTICED_DATE}}.

4. The said acts are without the Company's licence, consent or authority and amount to
   infringement of the Company's rights and to passing off, and are calculated to deceive
   members of the trade and the public into believing that your goods or business
   originate from or are approved by the Company. They cause the Company loss of goodwill
   and reputation which cannot be compensated in money alone.

5. You are hereby called upon to immediately CEASE AND DESIST from {{WHAT_MUST_STOP}}.

6. You are further called upon to confirm in writing, within {{UNDERTAKING_PERIOD}} from
   the date of receipt of this notice, that you have ceased the said acts and undertake
   not to resume them.

7. Should you fail to comply, the Company shall be constrained to initiate such civil and
   criminal proceedings as it may be advised, including for a permanent injunction,
   delivery-up and destruction of infringing material, damages or an account of profits,
   and costs, entirely at your risk as to the costs and consequences arising therefrom.

8. This notice is issued without prejudice to the Company's rights and remedies, all of
   which are expressly reserved, including the right to proceed without further notice.

{{SIGNATORY_BLOCK}}
```

## Mandatory checklist (run before delivering)
- [ ] The right asserted is identified and the basis on which CEAT holds it is stated.
- [ ] The offending acts are particularised — what, where, since when — not described in
      the abstract. "You are infringing our trademarks" is not a particular.
- [ ] Each act that must stop is spelt out, so compliance can be measured.
- [ ] A written undertaking is demanded, within a stated period running from receipt.
- [ ] Consequences of non-compliance stated.
- [ ] Rights-reserved / without-prejudice line present.
- [ ] `⚠ [VERIFY: the trademark registration number(s), class(es) and current status, and
      any surviving post-termination clause relied upon, against CEAT's own records.]`
- [ ] `⚠ [VERIFY: whether this notice should issue from CEAT or through its advocates.]`
- [ ] Draft marked "NON-STANDARD — for legal review"; review banner appended.

## Wording notes
- Where the infringer is an ex-dealer, the notice should also recite the termination —
  the date of the termination notice and the clause under which the right to use the
  marks fell away. Without that recital the ex-dealer's defence is that it was authorised.
- Where the goods are counterfeit rather than genuine goods sold outside the network, say
  so in terms. Those are different wrongs and attract different relief.
- Do not assert criminal liability under a specific section unless CEAT's counsel has
  settled which section applies on these facts.
